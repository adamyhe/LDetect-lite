# MacDonald2022 reproduction — findings

**Findings summary (current as of 2026-07-28, after the non-monotonic-map fixes below).** Distilled for human review — e.g. writing up the paper. Full investigation detail, diagnostic scripts, and dated process notes: `notes/logs/macdonald2022-pyrho-handoff.md`, `notes/logs/macdonald2022-boundary-diagnostics.md`, and `notes/logs/macdonald2022-interpolation-port.md`.

## Status

`examples/MacDonald2022` now has two distinct reproduction modes:

1. **deCODE/EUR replication.** Running with MacDonald's published deCODE maps and the legacy-compatible `scipy-periodic` filter reproduces the published deCODE/EUR block set essentially exactly. The only remaining structural mismatch is a tiny leading chr7 edge block (`31439-31443`) emitted by the original/legacy BED extraction convention but absent from MacDonald's published BED. All substantive internal boundaries match.
2. **pyrho published-map replication.** Running with MacDonald's published pyrho interpolated maps gave high but not exact reproduction as of 2026-07-26. A real, previously-undiscovered bug affecting covariance windowing on these maps (they're genuinely, severely non-monotonic) was found and fixed 2026-07-28 (Bug 1 below); a second fix (Bug 2, `inf`-poisoning) followed the same day. `pyrho_EUR`/`pyrho_AFR`/`pyrho_EAS` were re-run against Bug 1 on real data with a real improvement; `pyrho_SAS` was also re-run but remains uniformly poor for an unrelated reason (its `Ne` fallback — see below).

`pyrho_SAS` is essentially unchanged by Bug 2 (recall 0.429→0.429, Jaccard 0.275→0.276, bp-Jaccard 0.973→0.967 — within run-to-run noise, not a real shift). This is the expected null result, not a surprise: `pyrho_SAS`'s failure mode is dominated by its wrong `Ne` fallback (see below), a problem Bug 2 doesn't touch — Bug 2 only changes behavior for pairs whose exponent would otherwise overflow, which is a `Ne`-and-jump-size-dependent minority of pairs, not the systemic genome-wide bias a wrong `Ne` produces.

`pyrho_SAS`'s near-zero block-count `delta` and `mean bp-Jaccard=0.967` (comparable to the other three) alongside its collapsed recall/Jaccard confirm the *same* blocks exist in roughly the *same* places genome-wide, but internal boundary placement is systematically off everywhere — a different failure signature from the other three populations' localized outlier chromosomes. See "`pyrho_SAS`'s `Ne` fallback" below.

**Chr9 improved sharply in all three populations after Bug 2** — AFR chr9 Jaccard 0.600→0.944, EAS chr9 0.333→0.889, EUR chr9 0.820→0.906 — directly confirming the `inf`-poisoning hypothesis: chr9's desert contains the worst backward jumps on each map, so it's exactly where Bug 2's guard should matter most, and it did. But chr9 is no longer the worst chromosome anywhere: new or previously-secondary problem chromosomes are now the visible floor (AFR chr13, EAS chr4/chr1/chr19, EUR chr17/chr19), and EAS chr1 (Jaccard 0.431) wasn't flagged as a problem chromosome at all before this rerun. Aggregate movement is mixed, not uniformly better: AFR and EAS both improved (recall +0.007, +0.014; Jaccard +0.008, +0.010), but EUR's aggregate got very slightly *worse* (recall 0.951→0.947, Jaccard 0.920→0.913) despite chr9 improving — meaning Bug 2 shifted covariance values on other EUR chromosomes too (any non-monotonic map segment gets a per-pair `math.exp()` call instead of the old two-pointer-window value on some pairs), and for EUR specifically that net out slightly unfavorably outside chr9. This is consistent with mechanism 3 (razor-thin local-search margins, below) now doing more of the remaining work than the desert mechanism, rather than any indication Bug 2 itself is wrong — chr9's own sharp improvement is the direct, mechanistic confirmation of that fix.

These diagnostics annotate out-of-tolerance boundaries with mismatch class, local centromere context, local genetic-map density, and local SNP density. The downloaded `diagnostics/` bundle shows:

- Raw and final are nearly identical: 310 raw mismatch rows vs. 309 final rows across the seven target chromosomes.
- The dominant class is reciprocal shifted boundaries, not extra/missing splits: 262 shifted-boundary rows in both raw and final diagnostics.
- Every reported boundary lies exactly on a filtered SNP and genetic-map position (`nearest_snp_distance_bp=0`, `nearest_map_distance_bp=0` for all rows).
- Centromeres are not the broad explanation: final diagnostics have only 10/309 rows within 2 Mb of a centromere and none inside a centromere.
- Therefore the remaining pyrho divergence is a breakpoint-placement divergence that arises upstream of postprocessing.

The stage diagnostic compared 365 published internal boundaries against both the raw Fourier candidates and the local-search-refined (`fourier_ls`) loci. Only 35 were close at the raw Fourier stage, while 224 were close after local search. The residual 141 misses are mostly cases where local search improved a shifted raw candidate but did not get within 50 kb, or where it worsened an already shifted candidate. A smaller but cleaner subset has raw Fourier close to MacDonald's boundary and local search moving away.

The local-search replay diagnostic checked all 21 of those clean `local_search_moved_away` cases. In every case, MacDonald's boundary was inside the search window and exactly evaluated; replaying the actual `LocalSearch` class reproduced our recorded `fourier_ls` boundary and showed that the selected boundary was the true optimum under our objective. The MacDonald boundary was worse by only 0.003-0.172% in the metric, with median 0.0196%. This rules out a reachability, windowing, or replay mismatch bug and supports the near-tied-objective explanation.

## deCODE map interpolation bug: root cause found and fixed

`src/ldetect_lite/interpolate_maps.py::interpolate()` is a **point**-based linear interpolator (a port of `joepickrell/1000-genomes-genetic-maps`), but deCODE's source map is **interval**-based (`Begin, rate_cM_Mb, cumulative_cM_at_End` per interval, per MacDonald et al.'s `interpolate.R`). Feeding interval data through point-based interpolation is an off-by-one interval shift: every SNP's genetic position used the *next* interval's rate anchored at the *current* interval's endpoint, producing a ~0.001-0.01 cM error per SNP.

**Fix**: added `interpolate_intervals()` (a direct port of the R script's interval-anchoring logic) as an alternative to `interpolate()`, exposed via `ldetect interpolate-maps --mode {point,interval}` (default `point`, preserving prior behavior for true point-sampled maps like HapMap-interpolated 1000G maps). Confirmed on real deCODE data: interval mode is 10-50x closer to MacDonald's own published interpolated map than point mode (MAE 0.00004-0.0002 cM vs. 0.0017-0.002 cM, Pearson r = 1.0 in both).

Historical note: switching EUR from our recomputed interpolation to MacDonald's published already-interpolated deCODE map first resolved the bulk of the divergence. Subsequent legacy-compatibility fixes (`scipy-periodic` filtering and deCODE/EUR postprocessing parity) brought the current deCODE/EUR result to exact substantive boundary correspondence, aside from the tiny chr7 edge-block convention described above.

## Why the pyrho sets aren't exact anywhere

Unlike `examples/ldetect_original` (near-exact genome-wide, since it replays byte-identical archived VCF/map files the original authors used), the MacDonald pyrho block sets are high-concordance but not exact genome-wide. Current final runs have several exact chromosomes, but residual non-exact boundaries remain concentrated in a handful of chromosomes across all three populations. Three known, verified mechanisms explain much of this:

1. **Genetic-map deserts** (Category A — chr9, and 7 other chromosome/population combinations). Some chromosomes have large (tens-of-Mb) genetic-map dead zones (e.g. chr9's 17 Mb desert, consistent with its heterochromatic 9qh block). Legacy LDetect emits one unsplit block across the whole desert; `ldetect-lite` places an extra breakpoint inside it (same total genome-wide breakpoint budget, different placement) — confirmed directly against MacDonald's own git history (raw pre-centromere-removal BEDs).
2. **Non-monotonic genetic map bug in covariance windowing** (found 2026-07-28, fixed — see below). Silently corrupted the covariance pair window on ~10% of affected chromosomes' SNPs, up to 1047-SNP window-size errors on real data (chr9, the single worst `pyrho_AFR` chromosome). Not yet re-run against the fix at the time of writing; likely explains a meaningful share of what was previously attributed entirely to mechanism 3.
3. **Razor-thin local-search margins** (Category B — verified first on EAS chr4 and then across all 21 clean moved-away cases in the focused stage diagnostics). `LocalSearch` correctly finds the true optimum in its search window every time — no bug — but tiny, legitimate numerical differences in the covariance computation (which cannot be byte-compared against legacy — no published intermediates exist) are enough to flip which of two near-tied candidates wins a sub-0.1%-margin race. The replayed moved-away cases had MacDonald's boundary exactly reachable and evaluated, but worse than our chosen boundary by only 0.003-0.172% (median 0.0196%). This investigation (2026-07-04) checked window computation, metric formula, tie-breaking, and array/Decimal path equivalence, but never checked genetic-map monotonicity — a different "window" (the covariance pair window, not the local-search refinement window) that mechanism 2 above was quietly corrupting the whole time.

Mechanisms 1 and 3 converge with the same "flat region" finding documented for `ldetect_original` (see `notes/findings/ldetect-original-reproduction.md`) — this is the same underlying phenomenon showing up pervasively here rather than on 1-2 chromosomes, because MacDonald's pipeline (unlike `ldetect_original`) is a from-scratch rerun against modern VCF releases and pyrho/deCODE maps rather than a byte-identical replay.

Investigated and **ruled out** as explanations: postprocessing, boundary/SNP coordinate artifacts, local-search reachability/windowing, local-search replay mismatch, window-bound computation (matches legacy exactly), metric formula/tie-breaking (identical to legacy; two latent tie-break/denominator-guard bugs were found and fixed along the way, but don't affect any real reproduction output), the historical array-vs-Decimal local-search divergence (verified fixed — see `notes/logs/local-search-divergence-asn22.md`), `N_zero` denominator conditioning (essentially constant across search windows; the sensitivity comes from the numerator), MAF-filter type (`nref` vs. true minor-allele-frequency — tested genome-wide on EAS, makes concordance *worse*, not better), and individual-panel-membership drift (de-risked: selection method is a faithful, deterministic clone of MacDonald's documented recipe).

## Non-monotonic genetic map bug in covariance windowing (found and fixed, 2026-07-28)

MacDonald's own **published** pyrho interpolated maps (used directly by the active `pyrho_AFR`/`pyrho_EAS`/`pyrho_EUR`/`pyrho_SAS` block sets — not just the `macdonald_style` diagnostic below) are genuinely, severely non-monotonic in genetic position. Downloaded and checked directly: GWD chr9 (the single worst `pyrho_AFR` chromosome per the mismatch table above) has **18,130 backward jumps** (3.43% of its 527,983 rows), the worst a **-12.9 cM** jump against a **120.7 cM** total chromosome span. `config.yaml`'s own comments already noted this ("nonmonotone cM coordinates that original ldetect cannot process cleanly") in the context of *legacy* ldetect overflowing on it, but ldetect-lite's own covariance step was never checked for correctness under this condition — it doesn't crash, it silently computes a wrong result. Two distinct bugs were found, both real, both verified against the actual vendored/Bitbucket legacy source rather than assumed:

**Bug 1 — wrong covariance pair window.** `shrinkage.py::_genetic_stop_bounds_impl` computes each SNP's right-hand covariance pair window with a persistent "two-pointer" scan (`stop` never resets backward across outer iterations) — an O(n) amortized optimization that's only valid when genetic position is non-decreasing. Legacy's own equivalent loop (`P00_01_calc_covariance.py`) resets its scan to `j = i` fresh every row instead, so it has no persistent pointer to invalidate — verified byte-identical (modulo whitespace) against the real Bitbucket source at the repo's final commit. On real GWD chr9 data, this made the two computations disagree on **51,443 SNPs (9.74%)**, with window-size errors up to **1,047 SNPs** — always over-inclusion relative to the correct per-row scan, never under. This is a real, substantial, previously-undiscovered divergence from legacy behavior, not a tiny numerical artifact, and it has been silently present in every prior pyrho reproduction run (this two-pointer structure predates this investigation). Fixed by adding an `assume_monotonic` flag; `calc_covariance` detects non-monotonic genetic maps automatically (`np.diff(gpos_arr) >= 0`) and falls back to the legacy-faithful per-row fresh scan, emitting a stderr warning when it does. No new CLI flag; monotonic maps (the common case) keep the fast path. Regression test: `tests/test_shrinkage.py::test_genetic_stop_bounds_non_monotonic_map_needs_assume_monotonic_false`.

**Bug 2 — silent `inf` poisoning, and what "covariance overflows" in `config.yaml` actually means.** Asked directly whether bug 1's fix is *really* legacy's actual behavior, not an assumption — this surfaced a second, more serious issue. Legacy's per-pair loop computes `ee = math.exp(-4.0*NE*df/(2.0*len(inds)))` for the *value*, not just the window boundary. With a large enough backward jump, this exponent exceeds ~709 (float64's `exp()` overflow threshold) — with our assumed AFR `Ne=17469`, the real -12.9 cM chr9 jump gives exponent ≈882, which would overflow. In plain Python (legacy, and `shrinkage.py::partition_chromosome`'s own window-extension loop, which is not numba-compiled), `math.exp(882)` raises `OverflowError: math range error`. But `shrinkage.py::_shrink_ld_values` (the numba-compiled per-pair kernel) doesn't raise on the same overflow — LLVM/numba float arithmetic follows IEEE 754 and silently returns `+inf` instead, confirmed directly (`ds2 == inf`, no exception). An `inf` passes the `fabs(ds2) < cutoff` inclusion check and gets written straight into the covariance output, poisoning every downstream sum (matrix-to-vector, metric, local search) that touches that locus — silent corruption, which is strictly worse than a loud crash regardless of whether the crash itself is reachable in practice.

Asked directly whether the fix itself is legacy-compatible and could plausibly help, not just avoid a crash — the first version (zero out *any* negative `df`) was too aggressive and caught this: legacy has no special case for "negative `df`" at all, only for the specific point past which its own `math.exp()` call overflows. For the more common, *milder* non-monotonic-map cases (most of the 18,130 backward jumps on chr9 are presumably small, not -12.9 cM), the exponent is positive but stays well under ~709 — legacy would compute a real (if distorted-by-the-bad-map) `ee` and *include* that pair, not exclude it. Zeroing every negative-`df` pair regardless of magnitude, as the first version did, discarded real legacy-matching signal for the common case to guard against a failure mode that only applies to the rare extreme case. Narrowed the guard to check the actual exponent against the overflow threshold (`> 700.0`, a safe margin below the true ~709.78 boundary) rather than the sign of `df`: below it, `math.exp()` is called normally and matches legacy's own value exactly (negative `df` and all); only past it — where there genuinely is no legacy value to match, since legacy itself would crash — is the pair treated as "no reliable decay information" (`ds2 = 0.0`). `partition_chromosome`'s extension loop needed no equivalent narrowing: it only makes a boolean stop/continue decision, and legacy's own code continues extending for *any* negative `df` regardless of magnitude (a huge `rho` is never `< cutoff`), so "keep extending" was already correct across the whole range, not just the overflow case.

This means Bug 2's fix is now more than a pure robustness patch — for the likely-common mild-jump case, it should reproduce legacy's actual (bug-for-bug) covariance value, which could plausibly *improve* alignment with the published blocks rather than just avoid corruption, though this is still only checked in isolation (see below), not against real block output. Regression tests: `tests/test_shrinkage.py::test_shrink_ld_values_negative_df_returns_zero_not_inf` (confirms the true-overflow case is still zeroed, not `inf`) and `test_shrink_ld_values_mild_negative_df_matches_legacy_not_zeroed` (confirms a non-overflowing negative `df` now matches legacy's real formula, not zero) and `test_partition_chromosome_survives_backward_jump_past_chunk_boundary`.

**Open question, deferred to the authors: does MacDonald's actual run ever hit this overflow at all?** Initially reasoned that `config.yaml`'s "original ldetect covariance overflows" comment must be this exact `OverflowError`, and that legacy therefore has no answer to reproduce for the worst chr9 pairs. Two follow-up checks complicate that:
- A 26.46 Mb gap in MacDonald's *published* AFR chr9 blocks (42.64–69.10 Mb, squarely containing the -12.9 cM jump) initially looked like confirmation of a crash. It isn't: MacDonald's README says they delete *any entire block* that overlaps *any portion* of a centromere (chr9's GRCh38 centromere, 43.39–45.52 Mb, is only 2.13 Mb — far smaller than the gap). The pre-existing "Category A" finding above (verified against MacDonald's own pre-centromere-removal git history) already establishes that legacy computes one large unsplit block across genetic-map deserts like this one; deleting that single large block for centromere overlap fully explains the gap with no crash required. Retracting the crash-gap claim.
- Tested whether MacDonald might use a single uniform `Ne=11418` (their only documented example, for EUR) across all populations rather than population-specific values, since `Ne=11418` keeps the same chr9 exponent under the overflow threshold (≈576 instead of ≈882). Real cluster data argues against this: `pyrho_SAS` already uses the `Ne=11418` fallback (no SAS-specific value is documented anywhere — see below) and it performs uniformly poorly across every single chromosome (recall 0.31-0.61 genome-wide), unlike EUR/AFR/EAS's mostly-exact-except-a-few-chromosomes pattern. Population-specific `Ne` is already established as necessary and correct (see the "Ruled out" list above); a uniform fallback breaks results broadly rather than fixing this one edge case.

**Would legacy actually crash on the real published maps?** Checked this directly across all three populations with an already-settled/confirmed `Ne` (AFR/EAS's population-specific values are the same ones already established as correct for `ldetect_original`; EUR's `Ne=11418` is MacDonald's own literally-documented value, not an assumption). Downloaded each population's own map-source chr9 file (GWD/CHB/IBS) and computed the worst backward jump's exponent at that population's real `Ne`:

| pop (map) | `Ne` used | worst chr9 jump | exponent | overflows `math.exp()`? |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | :---: |
| AFR (GWD) | 17,469 | -12.9 cM | 881.9 | Yes |
| EAS (CHB) | 14,269 | -20.5 cM | 1,137.0 | Yes |
| EUR (IBS) | 11,418 (confirmed, not assumed) | -21.9 cM | 1,201.9 | Yes |

All three overflow — this doesn't hinge on the AFR `Ne` question at all, since EUR's value is directly confirmed from MacDonald's own README and still overflows by a wide margin (492 past the ~709.78 threshold). (`pyrho_SAS` is excluded from this table: there is no settled or even reasonably-guessed `Ne` for it — see below — so no valid comparison can be made yet, and none should be assumed.) This makes it very likely that legacy's own pipeline hit a real failure on chr9 for every population tested, which would make the "Category A: legacy emits one unsplit block across the desert" pattern a symptom of that failure rather than (or in addition to) a purely biological low-recombination-density explanation. Still not proof of what actually happened inside MacDonald's own run — genuinely unresolved, and the user is contacting MacDonald et al. directly to confirm the actual per-population `Ne` values used and whether they ever observed a crash or failed job on chr9. The two code fixes above stand on their own regardless of the answer.

**Re-verified against real block output for all four populations (2026-07-28)** — see the comparison table above. `pyrho_SAS` was re-run too and is unaffected, as expected given its failure mode is the unrelated `Ne` question, not anything Bug 2 addresses. No other pipeline stage uses genetic position at all (matrix-to-vector, metric, local search, and filtering all operate on physical positions and already-computed covariance values), so these two fixes are the complete set needed for the non-monotonic-map issue.

Also wired up `pyrho_{AFR,EAS,EUR,SAS}_macdonald_style` block sets (`config.yaml`), using `interpolate_macdonald_pyrho()` (already implemented, previously unwired to any block set) to re-interpolate the *raw* pyrho rate maps with MacDonald's own `interpolate_pyhro.R` bugs deliberately reproduced, rather than using their already-interpolated published output directly. This is a diagnostic comparison target: if it agrees closely with the `published`-mode results (now that both go through the same fixed covariance code), that's independent confirmation we understand MacDonald's interpolation bug correctly.

## `pyrho_SAS`'s `Ne` fallback: deprioritized, open, pending author contact (2026-07-28)

`pyrho_SAS` uses `Ne=11418` (the EUR/CEU value) as a fallback because no SAS-specific value is documented anywhere — a gap already flagged in `notes/logs/macdonald2022-pyrho-handoff.md` before this session. Real cluster data confirms this fallback is likely wrong: `pyrho_SAS` shows uniformly poor recall (0.31-0.61) across *every* chromosome, unlike EUR/AFR/EAS's mostly-exact pattern with a handful of specific problem chromosomes — a qualitatively different, genome-wide-bias failure mode consistent with a wrong LD-decay parameter rather than the same desert/near-tie mechanisms affecting the other three populations.

Searched for a literature-backed South Asian/GIH-specific value calibrated the same way as the existing `CEU=11418`/`YRI=17469`/`CHB+JPT=14269` (traced to SHAPEIT documentation, itself from IMPUTE2/Myers-McVean-era HapMap Phase II recombination-rate-based Ne estimation — a population set that never included South Asians). Checked the pyrho paper itself (Spence & Song 2019, the same method behind the raw maps `pyrho_SAS` uses), since it reports `smc++`-inferred population size histories for all 26 1000 Genomes populations including GIH (`smcpp_popsizes_1kg.csv` in the `popgenmethods/pyrho` GitHub repo: GIH ≈72,098 at present). Not usable as a substitute: cross-checking the same populations both ways shows `smc++`'s demographic-history Ne and SHAPEIT's LD-decay-tuned Ne aren't just different scales (10-20x apart) but different *orderings* (`smc++`: CEU≈223,180 > YRI≈101,323; SHAPEIT: CEU=11,418 < YRI=17,469) — they measure different things and don't rescale into each other. No other source found calibrated the same way as the existing three values.

**Deprioritized per user direction (2026-07-28)** — the user is writing to MacDonald et al. directly to confirm what `Ne` (if any) they actually used for AFR/EAS/SAS. Leave `config.yaml` as-is until that's answered; don't guess further.

## Bottom line

The razor-thin-margin explanation (mechanism 3) was investigated thoroughly and, now that both non-monotonic-map fixes have been re-verified against real EUR/AFR/EAS reruns, it's the best remaining explanation for the divergence that's left: chr9's desert-driven overflow (mechanism 2) is fixed and its own boundaries improved sharply as a direct result, but the new/persisting worst chromosomes (AFR chr13, EAS chr1/chr4/chr19, EUR chr17/chr19) sit outside that mechanism entirely. Do not add a deadband or stability heuristic to coerce near ties toward MacDonald's published boundaries; that would make replication diagnostics look better by changing the algorithm's decision rule. Current state (mean Jaccard 0.876-0.963 across AFR/EAS/EUR) is close to the practical ceiling for an independent, from-scratch reproduction against modern data releases without MacDonald's own covariance intermediates, which don't exist publicly. `pyrho_SAS` remains the one open item, gated on the `Ne` question below rather than on anything left to fix in the covariance code.
